Grade 5 Science Reference Material

Unit: Electricity and Safety

Learning Objectives:
- Define electricity in simple terms.
- Identify basic sources of electricity used at home and school.
- Distinguish between conductors and insulators.
- Understand the purpose of switches, wires, plugs, and bulbs in a simple circuit.
- Recognize common electrical safety rules.

Key Concepts:
1. Electricity is a form of energy that helps devices and appliances work.
2. A simple electric circuit includes a cell or battery, wires, a switch, and a bulb.
3. Conductors allow electricity to pass through them. Examples: copper, aluminium.
4. Insulators do not allow electricity to pass through them easily. Examples: rubber, plastic, wood.
5. A switch is used to open or close a circuit.

Electrical Safety Rules:
- Never touch switches or plugs with wet hands.
- Do not insert fingers or metal objects into sockets.
- Do not use damaged wires or broken plugs.
- Switch off appliances when not in use.
- Ask an adult for help when handling electrical devices.

Suggested Question Areas:
- Definitions of electricity, conductor, insulator, and circuit.
- Difference between open and closed circuits.
- Examples of conductors and insulators from daily life.
- Short answers about electrical safety at home and school.
- Diagram-based questions on simple electric circuits.
